Varroa Treatment Calendar (UK)

Varroa Treatment Calendar UK – Seasonal Timing & Schedule

Last updated: 17 July 2026

Knowing when to treat varroa mites is one of the most important decisions a UK beekeeper makes. This varroa treatment calendar provides a practical seasonal schedule for monitoring mite levels, choosing an appropriate treatment window and checking whether control has been successful.

When should you treat varroa in the UK?

There is no single treatment date that suits every colony. Timing should be based on monitoring results, local conditions, the amount of sealed brood present and the instructions for the treatment being used. Start with our guide to varroa monitoring methods rather than relying only on the time of year.

For many UK beekeepers, the main treatment window is during late summer, after honey supers have been removed and before large numbers of winter bees are raised. Earlier monitoring and follow-up checks remain important, particularly around July, August and September.

Use this schedule alongside the wider Year in the Apiary guide so that varroa control fits naturally into the rest of your colony management.

Quick links: start with Varroa Management, then use the specialist pages: monitoring methods, chemical treatments, non-chemical methods and PPE & safety. Printable PDFs are on Free Downloads.
Seasonal flow: for many UK beekeepers, the most important path is July varroa monitoring → August treatment period → September follow-up → autumn colony preparation in October.

How to plan a UK varroa treatment schedule

There isn't one perfect calendar for every apiary. A useful varroa treatment schedule is built around:

  • Monitoring: use a consistent monitoring method to identify trends rather than treating only because a particular month has arrived.
  • Season: late summer varroa control is especially important because high mite and virus pressure can damage the bees that must survive through winter.
  • Brood: when sealed brood is present, many mites are reproducing inside capped cells and may be harder for some treatments to reach.
  • Colony condition: visible symptoms of varroa infestation may indicate that mite and virus pressure is already affecting the colony, although monitoring remains important because serious infestations are not always obvious.
  • Treatment choice: compare the seasonal limitations of chemical treatments with suitable non-chemical methods.
  • Safety: always follow the product label and use appropriate PPE for varroa treatments.
Why timing matters: the aim is to reduce mite levels before winter bees are raised and then confirm that control has worked. Waiting until a colony shows advanced signs of varroa collapse may leave too little time for it to recover.
Important: this page is a planning guide. It does not replace product instructions or current UK advice from BeeBase and the National Bee Unit. Temperature ranges, treatment duration and honey-super restrictions vary between products.

Example varroa calendar for UK beekeepers

This example is designed to help you think through timing across the year, including spring monitoring and control, the main late-summer treatment window and possible winter broodless periods. Adjust the schedule for your local conditions, colony strength, monitoring results and the treatment being used.

Example UK varroa monitoring & treatment calendar (planning guide)
Season What to do Why it matters Helpful links
Spring (build-up) Monitor trends; consider biotechnical options if suitable (e.g. drone brood trapping). Early control can reduce the rate of population growth into summer. Monitoring • Non-chemical IPM • Year in the Apiary
Summer (honey flow) Keep monitoring; avoid treatments that conflict with supers or conditions. Maintains awareness while keeping honey handling clean and safe. July monitoring • Varroa symptoms • Late-summer planning
Late summer / early autumn Main treatment window after supers are removed; re-check afterwards. Protects the winter bee generation from high mite/virus pressure. Late Summer Varroa • August treatment period • Chemical treatments • PPE
Autumn (before winter) Confirm mite levels are under control; tidy records and plan next season. Prevents a "quiet failure" where colonies look fine but are carrying damaging mite loads. September follow-up • Autumn colony preparation • Medicine records • Downloads
Winter (broodless periods) Where appropriate, consider a broodless-window approach and record outcomes. Some strategies work best when little/no sealed brood is present. Monitoring • Seasonal treatment options • Medicine records

How to choose a plan that fits your apiary

Your treatment schedule should follow a simple monitor → act → confirm cycle. Begin with reliable varroa monitoring, select an appropriate control method and then check the colony again after treatment.

Monitor → Act → Confirm
  1. Monitor: use one consistent method to assess mite levels and identify changes.
  2. Act: choose a suitable treatment or control method based on the result, season and colony condition.
  3. Confirm: monitor again after the treatment window to check that mite levels have reduced.

Use a mix of approaches where appropriate:

  • See non-chemical IPM methods for biotechnical options.
  • See chemical treatment types for seasonal constraints and resistance awareness.
  • Use PPE and safety guidance whenever handling treatments.

Record keeping and downloads

Keeping records makes your calendar easier next year. Record:

  • Monitoring method and result
  • Treatment type used (if any), dates and notes
  • Any withdrawal period / honey handling notes
Printable PDFs: grab the checklists and summaries on Free Downloads, and keep your veterinary medicine records up to date so you are recording treatments properly alongside your monitoring notes.
